4th Century (300s)

Early trade networks and regional kingdoms establish foundational exchange systems across West African routes.

5th Century (400s)

Roman decline; early monastic centers become focal points for preserving classical knowledge.

6th Century (500s)

Gold trade expands; oral traditions maintain sophisticated administrative and technical knowledge systems.

7th Century (600s)

Merovingian scriptoria emerge; monks copy and preserve texts in isolated monastic communities.

8th Century (700s)

Trans-Saharan trade routes flourish; knowledge of astronomy, mathematics, and crafts spreads rapidly.

9th Century (800s)

Charlemagne's renaissance; cathedral schools and manuscript production increase scholarly activity.

10th Century (900s)

Mali and Ghana empires establish centers of learning; scholars and merchants exchange ideas across continents.

11th Century (1000s)

Early universities form; intellectual exchange strengthens through pilgrimage and trade networks.

12th Century (1100s)

Timbuktu emerges as a major intellectual hub; Islamic scholarship flourishes across West Africa.

13th Century (1200s)

Sorbonne founded; scholasticism peaks; translation movements connect European and Islamic knowledge.

14th Century (1300s)

Mali's golden age; universities in Timbuktu attract scholars from across Africa and beyond.

15th Century (1400s)

Gutenberg's press invented; Renaissance begins; manuscript culture transitions to print.

16th Century (1500s)

Atlantic trade begins; knowledge systems adapt; oral traditions remain primary scholarly method.

17th Century (1600s)

Academies and salons flourish; scientific societies emerge; peer review systems formalize.

18th Century (1700s)

Trade intensifies; knowledge exchanges accelerate through merchant networks and diplomacy.

19th Century (1800s)

Industrial revolution; mass printing; encyclopedias and periodicals democratize knowledge access.

20th Century (1900s)

Independence movements; universities established; African scholarship gains global recognition.

21st Century (2000s — Present)

Digital revolution; online platforms enable real-time global collaboration and instant knowledge sharing.
